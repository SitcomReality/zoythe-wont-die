The narrative is a bit too rigid and in particular, the specifics of the story are too consistent. We want to add more randomness and variation to the potential storylines.

Foreshadowing! If there's a twist or unexpected outcome, it needs to be foreshadowed. Our prompting system should include a reminder to foreshadow relevant things at suitable times.

The context, generally, needs to contain a bit more context and knowledge of specific details to make each story more coherent.

In the call to adventure, the player always sees their own body with a weapon embedded in it. But maybe sometimes the player should see a manner of death like being pushed from a high place or killed in another way that doesn't involve an immediately apparent weapon. Perhaps the player could have died to poison or something else that leaves no obvious clues and could require the player to investigate their corpse further to reveal a clue to how they died. And the story should also be flexible enough to allow the player to both discover or fail to discover clues like this if they investigate the right thing. If the player chooses to investigate, pry, or to consider things further, then generally that should mean they stay in the current beat for longer, unless the beat is intended to transition via the player's investigation - or whatever action the player made. So if the player in the call to adventure beat "sees a wise man approaching" (the intended mentor), and the player chooses "run the other direction", then that could prevent the story from advancing to the meeting the mentor beat.

The meeting the mentor prompt is too vague and also limiting. We should provide some more specific detail that can be randomized. Maybe sometimes the mentor is a ghost of an ancestor. Also the beat ends too quickly no matter what the player does. The player should be able to be skeptical about the mentor and ask the mentor questions for a while before being railroaded, but there should also always (usually?) be a button that conveys "end this beat and move on".

Road of tests needs terminology variation, and it also needs to say to use specific details from the context in its prompt. Eg. the location should be something generated at the start and preserved in/pulled from the context.

The Ordeal shouldn't be an accusation scene like it is. Or maybe sometimes it could be, but the story shouldn't necessarily always force the player to make this kind of choice. If the player has failed to uncover enough clues, the story should allow the player to have their killer revealed to them, rather than always insisting there's a choice made. It should be possible for the story to play out as a long, dramatic murder-mystery with an eventual reveal, but it should also be possible for the player to advance the story faster by uncovering and using clues.

The resurrection beat needs more specifity and variety, including to allow for a final twist. Sometimes, it might turn out that the 'murderer' that seemed to be revealed during The Ordeal was innocent, and the real murderer was actually the player's companion throughout the journey. The other beats need similar enhancements to make them more interesting, more coherent within a single story, and more distinct between different stories.

In any context where it's relevant, if the player knows a clue that's useful for uncovering more information, then one of the buttons should involve using that clue. Sometimes, there should be red herring buttons that involve using clues or red herring clues.

Character details should be less hard-coded and more randomized or generated. We should have a list of names we can randomly use, and traits/qualities that can be randomly assigned or selected based on what the story needs. And/or we should use AI-generated character names and details.

Sometimes additional clues might be uncovered, or sometimes it might lead the player astray or cause unintended negative consequences.

There needs to be some more overt 'consequences of decisions made earlier', with more unique specific details tracked in the memory context that can be used to influence later story beats.

The railroading should be loosened up a little more to provide more flexibility for players to experience the setting of their current beat, or to explore or investigate further before moving on.


Currently the story always describes the protagonist's death as being directly, and maybe causally, related to the demise of the death god, but this shouldn't always be the case.

On the new game screen, there should be an option to select the story's 'scope', either leaving it random, or choosing one of four options for "how vast or wide-reaching the consequences of the player's actions might be".

We should be able to tell a story with these four distinct levels of "grandiosity"/stakes:
	1. Intimate (a story that involves only the protagonists immediate community: family, friends, neighbours. The most powerful/authorative characters would be patriarchs/matriarchs, community leaders)
	2. Local/Regional (The most powerful/authorative characters would be mayors, priests/bishops, aristocrats)
	3. National/Global (the most powerful/authorative characters would be presidents, royalty, pope-level religious leaders, military generals)
	4. Cosmic (a story that has the protagonist directly involved in the affairs and fates of gods. Likely to involve immortal characters with inconceivable divine powers.)
